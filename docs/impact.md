Who benefits?

URIMAI is designed for people who often struggle to access government support because of language barriers, limited literacy, age or lack of digital skills. This includes elderly people, widows, low-income families, informal workers and people with disabilities, particularly in rural and semi-urban India.
We would initially aim to pilot URIMAI with around 100–200 citizens in Tamil Nadu, focusing on people who have difficulty discovering or applying for government benefits. Over time, the same approach could serve millions of underserved citizens across different states and languages.

What changes?

Today, people may not know which government benefits they are entitled to. Even when they discover a relevant scheme, complicated eligibility rules, documentation requirements and application forms can prevent them from completing the process. Many depend on relatives, intermediaries or repeated visits to service centres for help.
URIMAI changes this by allowing people to explain their circumstances in their own language and receive step-by-step assistance through conversation.
Instead of simply receiving a list of schemes, they understand which benefits may apply to them, what documents they need, how to complete application forms and what to do next. They can speak their answers instead of struggling with complicated forms. If they stop midway, URIMAI saves their progress and helps them continue.
The intended impact is greater independence, fewer avoidable barriers and more people able to pursue the support available to them.

How would we know it worked?

We would evaluate the pilot using real users and measure:
Independent access: At least 80% of users complete benefit discovery without another person's assistance.
Discovery accuracy: At least 90% of recommendations match manually verified scheme eligibility rules.
Understanding: At least 80% of users correctly understand why a scheme may apply and what they need to do next.
Application progress: At least 50% of users who discover a potentially relevant scheme progress to application submission.
Journey continuity: At least 70% of users who return to an incomplete journey successfully complete their next pending action.
These are initial targets to validate through the pilot, not existing results.

Main risk

The biggest risk is providing incorrect or outdated government scheme information, which could mislead people who depend on this support. Eligibility rules, required documents and application procedures can change, and actual submission or status tracking may require government integrations.
URIMAI must therefore rely on verified official sources, clearly communicate uncertainty and never guarantee eligibility or approval. Where digital submission is unavailable, it must guide users through the appropriate official channel.
